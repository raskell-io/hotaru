What you're watching

Each point is a neuron, and each flash is a spike. Neurons connect mostly to their neighbours, so activity spreads as a wave. A burst ends when the synapses run out of resources, and the culture stays quiet for a few seconds while they recover. Real cultures on multi-electrode arrays show the same rhythm. Slow the speed down to 0.1× to watch a burst travel.

The model

  • Izhikevich neurons: 80% excitatory (regular spiking and chattering), 20% fast-spiking inhibitory.
  • 100 synapses each, mostly within ~150 µm, with delays set by distance at 150 µm/ms.
  • Short-term synaptic depression (Tsodyks–Markram), which shapes the bursts.
  • Sparse random input at 0.1 Hz per neuron, like spontaneous release.
  • Deterministic: a seed always grows the same culture.

The Pong experiment

The same cultures can also play Pong through their electrodes, following the DishBrain protocol: predictable feedback when they hit the ball, noise when they miss. A control culture with random feedback plays next to them. Run the experiment.

Izhikevich (2003), Simple model of spiking neurons. Tsodyks & Markram (1997). Kagan et al. (2022), In vitro neurons learn and exhibit sentience when embodied in a simulated game-world. Sinapayen, Masumori & Ikegami (2017), Learning by stimulation avoidance.
